Supermarket simulator on iPhone: run a grocery store that can spoil

A good supermarket sim is not about scanning barcodes. It is about the decisions a grocer makes: what to stock, how much, at what price, and what to do before the milk goes off.

Updated 2026-11-12

What makes a supermarket different from a shop

A clothing store sells a few things at a fat margin. A supermarket sells many cheap things to many people. That changes how you play:

  • Volume over margin. Each item earns little, so footfall and basket size matter more than any single price.
  • Food spoils. Milk, bread and fruit only last a few days. Order too much and you throw money away at closing.
  • Cold storage is a limit. Fresh goods need fridge space, so the fridges cap how much you can keep.
  • The checkout is the bottleneck. Big baskets take longer to ring up, so staff and registers decide how many shoppers you can serve.

The supermarket in City Ambitions

From version 1.2 you can open a supermarket as one of your businesses in New York. It sells everyday groceries, from milk and eggs to bread, fruit, pasta, cereal and frozen pizza. Every product has its own price, and the Daily Close shows what sold, what it cost and, new for groceries, what spoiled.

A supermarket pulls far more shoppers than a boutique (the one in the screenshot serves 345 a day), but each sale is small. The skill is keeping the shelves full of what moves without over-ordering what rots.

Tips for running a grocery store well

  1. Order fresh goods little and often. A short shelf life punishes big orders. Let longer-lasting lines carry the big orders instead.
  2. Price staples close to market. In a high-volume store a small discount on a basic can bring a lot of extra baskets; check the next Daily Close before you go further.
  3. Watch the spoiled line. If the same product spoils every few days, order less of it or price it to move.
  4. Staff for the crowd. If shoppers give up in the queue, hire for another block before you cut prices. See staff and opening hours.
  5. Use delivery contracts. A supplier can top up your shelves every morning so you never forget an order.

Is it a real simulator or an idle game?

City Ambitions has no timers to wait on and no automatic money. Demand comes from your prices, stock, staff, opening hours and the regulars you earn, so a badly run supermarket loses money. If you want the full picture of how demand works, read how pricing works and regulars and Store Pulse.
