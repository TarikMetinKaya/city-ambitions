How to run a store in a business sim: pricing, stock and staff tips

Every shop in a business sim comes down to three questions: what to charge, how much to keep on the shelf, and how many people you need at the till. Get those right and the rest is growth.

Updated 2026-11-12

1. Pricing: start at market, then listen

Most business sims give each product a market price: what shoppers expect to pay. Start there. Raise the price a little and watch two things: how many shoppers walk away, and whether profit per day goes up.

  • Pricing below market rarely pays unless you are clearing stock or your store has spare capacity.
  • Pricing above market works when you cannot serve everyone anyway: the shoppers you lose were going to leave the queue.
  • Change one price at a time and compare the next day's results.

In City Ambitions the numbers behind this are public: see how pricing works for the exact price curve.

2. Stock: never run the shelf empty

An empty shelf loses today's sale and, in most sims, tomorrow's customer. A full warehouse ties up cash. Aim in between:

  • Keep at least one day of sales on the shelf, plus a margin for busy days.
  • Order before you run out, not after: deliveries take time.
  • Buy in bulk only when the unit cost drops enough to pay for the storage.
  • For perishable goods, order little and often.

More detail: stock, storage and containers.

3. Staff: the hidden cap on sales

Customers who cannot be served leave. If your shop is busy but profit is flat, the till is probably the limit.

  • Staff the busiest hours first; nights and early mornings usually come last.
  • Watch morale and raise requests before people quit.
  • When the queue is the limit, a higher price can earn more than another hire.

More detail: staff and opening hours.

4. Read the books every day

The daily report is the best teacher in any business sim. Revenue, cost of goods, wages, rent and tax tell you which decision worked. In City Ambitions this is the Daily Close at midnight.

5. Grow when one store is boring

When a store runs itself, open the next one: a new neighborhood, a new kind of business (a supermarket or fast food from version 1.2), a container import to lower costs, or a factory to make your own goods.
